Chapter Seven, Section Three

Titles: Text+, Templates, and Fusion Titles

Part VII of XVI
Category Practical
Sheet 041 / 095
Depends On 7.2, 1.3
Read Time ~7 Min

Putting text on screen in Resolve isn't a single tool with one workflow — it's three distinct tools, each trading speed against control differently, and reaching for the wrong one is a common source of wasted time in both directions: building something elaborate by hand that a template already offers, or fighting a template's limits to get an effect it was never designed for. This chapter is about knowing which of the three actually matches a given title's needs.

Text+: Fast, Direct, Good Enough for Most Titles

Text+ is the default text generator, and it covers the large majority of what most projects actually need: typed text with direct control over font, size, color, position, and basic animation, all adjusted through the Inspector without ever leaving the Edit page. A lower-third with a name and title, an on-screen caption, a simple credit — Text+ handles all of these quickly, and its speed comes specifically from not requiring the heavier machinery either of the other two tools involves. For a large share of titling work, reaching for anything more elaborate than Text+ is solving a problem the moment doesn't actually have.

Templates: Designed Titles You Didn't Have to Design

Templates are pre-built title designs, drawn from Resolve's included library or downloaded separately, that arrive with their look, animation, and layout already decided — you're supplying the content, not designing the presentation. This is the right tool specifically when a project needs a consistent, polished look applied repeatedly across many similar title cards — a series of lower thirds for different interview subjects, say — where hand-building each one in Text+ would mean redoing the same design decisions dozens of times. The tradeoff is real: a template's design is largely fixed, and pushing it meaningfully outside what it was built to do tends to fight the tool rather than cooperate with it.

A template saves you from making the same design decision repeatedly. It doesn't save you from a decision the template's designer never anticipated — that's a different tool's job.

Fusion Titles: Building the Thing Yourself

When a title needs behavior that neither Text+'s direct controls nor an existing template's fixed design can produce — genuinely custom animation, multiple interacting elements, effects built from scratch — the answer is a Fusion title, built using the same node-based logic Chapter 1.3 introduced. This isn't a separate, title-specific system; it's the identical node graph approach the rest of Resolve's more advanced work runs on, applied here to generating type and motion instead of grading color. That shared foundation is exactly why Chapter 1.3 spent time establishing node thinking as a concept before any specific tool used it — the investment pays off again here, in a completely different corner of the application.

Escalating Only as Far as the Title Actually Needs

The practical discipline worth carrying forward is resisting the urge to escalate by default. Most titles genuinely are Text+ jobs, and treating them that way is faster, not lazier. Reach for a template when a look needs to repeat consistently across several title cards. Reach for Fusion only when the other two have a specific, identifiable limit the title actually needs to cross — custom behavior neither direct controls nor a fixed design can express. Matching the tool to the actual requirement, rather than defaulting to whichever tool feels most capable, is what keeps titling work fast across a whole project instead of only on its simplest cards.

Chapter 7.4 stays on the Edit page but broadens the lens from titles specifically to generators and Fusion effects more generally — the wider category of on-timeline tools that titles turn out to be one specific case of.

Key Concepts to Remember

  • Text+ is fast, direct, and correct for the large majority of ordinary titling needs — no reason to reach further by default.
  • Templates trade fixed design for consistency across repeated title cards — powerful for repetition, limited for customization.
  • Fusion titles use the same node-based logic from Chapter 1.3, applied to type and motion instead of color grading.
  • Escalate tools only as far as a title's actual requirement crosses — matching the tool to the need keeps titling work fast overall.
