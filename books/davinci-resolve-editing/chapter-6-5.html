Group Clips as an Alternative to Multicam

Part VI of XVI
Category Practical
Sheet 038 / 095
Depends On 6.4
Read Time ~6 Min

Everything Part VI has covered so far — the synced clip, the live viewer, the audio routing — is real infrastructure, built for a specific situation: multiple cameras capturing one continuous, unrepeatable moment at once. Not every situation that feels multicam-shaped actually is that. This closing chapter of Part VI covers Group Clips, a lighter structure for a genuinely different problem that a full multicam clip would solve, but would over-solve.

What a Group Clip Actually Is

A Group Clip bundles several clips into one object you can switch between, the same basic idea as a multicam clip's angle property. What it doesn't include is a multicam clip's defining feature: the dedicated viewer showing every grouped clip simultaneously, built for switching between them live during playback. A Group Clip's switching happens differently — selecting which of the grouped clips is active from a list, typically in the Inspector, rather than watching several play back together and choosing in real time. It's a simpler mechanism for a simpler need.

When Multicam Is Genuinely Overkill

The situation Group Clips are actually built for is alternate takes, not simultaneous angles: several different recordings of the same performance, shot one after another rather than at the same time. A host recording several takes of the same scripted line, or several passes at the same demonstration, produces clips that are related in content but never overlap in time — there's no shared instant for a Sync Bin or a multicam clip's timecode-and-waveform machinery to align them against, because there isn't one. Building a full multicam clip for this case doesn't just add unnecessary setup; it doesn't really apply, since multicam's entire value proposition assumes simultaneity that alternate takes don't have.

Multicam solves synchronized simultaneity. Group Clips solve related alternatives. Reaching for the heavier tool when the lighter one actually matches the problem is effort spent solving a kind of alignment your footage never needed in the first place.

The Real Difference Is the Missing Live Viewer

It's worth being precise about why this distinction matters practically, not just conceptually. A live, real-time multi-angle viewer is only useful when watching several sources unfold together, at the same moment, tells you something a list of separate options can't — which is exactly the case for synchronized angles of one live event, and exactly not the case for alternate takes recorded separately, where there's no shared timeline for simultaneous viewing to reveal anything. Choosing between takes is fundamentally a comparison decision, better served by reviewing candidates individually — Chapter 3.2's Source Tape is often the faster tool for that specific job — and a Group Clip's simple switching is what lets you apply whichever take you settle on across every place it's been used, without redoing that placement by hand.

Choosing Between the Two, Going Forward

The rule worth carrying forward, closing out this part: reach for a multicam clip when multiple sources are capturing one continuous, simultaneous moment and you want to cut between them live. Reach for a Group Clip when you have several related but non-simultaneous options and you want the freedom to swap which one is active without manually replacing it everywhere it appears. Both solve real problems; neither is a lesser version of the other, and knowing which one your footage actually calls for is the practical skill this closing chapter has been building toward.

Key Concepts to Remember

  • A Group Clip bundles clips into one switchable object, but without multicam's live, simultaneous multi-angle viewer.
  • Group Clips fit alternate, non-simultaneous takes — footage with no shared instant for multicam's sync methods to align against.
  • A live multi-angle viewer only earns its keep when sources unfold together in real time — not when comparing separate, non-simultaneous options.
  • Multicam solves synchronized simultaneity; Group Clips solve related alternatives — neither is a lesser version of the other.
