Chapter Seven, Section Four

Generators and Fusion Effects on the Edit Page

Part VII of XVI
Category Practical
Sheet 042 / 095
Depends On 7.3
Read Time ~6 Min

Chapter 7.3 closed on a small but useful reframe: a title isn't really its own category of thing — it's one specific case of a broader set of tools, generators and Fusion effects, that can live directly on the Edit page timeline. This chapter widens the lens to that full category, because Text+ and Fusion titles turn out to be the tip of a larger, genuinely useful iceberg.

What a Generator Actually Is

A generator is any timeline element that isn't sourced from footage at all — a solid color, a gradient, a noise pattern, a countdown, and yes, a Text+ title, since Text+ is itself technically a generator rather than a separate category of tool. Every generator places onto the timeline exactly like a clip does — trimmed, positioned, layered on tracks — but with no underlying media file behind it, generated entirely by Resolve at render time. Recognizing Text+ as one member of this family rather than something apart from it is useful precisely because it means everything about placing and trimming generators already applies to titles, and vice versa.

Fusion Effects Without Leaving the Edit Page

Alongside generators, the Effects Library also offers Fusion-built effects that apply directly to an existing clip on the Edit page — blurs, glows, and various composite looks — without requiring a visit to the dedicated Fusion page at all. These are genuinely built from the same node technology Chapter 1.3 introduced and Chapter 7.3 connected to Fusion titles; they're simply packaged as single, ready-to-apply effects rather than exposed as an editable node graph. Dragging one onto a clip and adjusting its exposed Inspector controls is often all a shot needs, and it's considerably faster than opening a full Fusion composition for an effect this contained.

A Fusion effect on the Edit page is the same underlying technology as a full Fusion composition, pre-built and simplified down to a few Inspector sliders. Reach for the packaged version until a shot's needs genuinely exceed what it exposes.

When to Drop Into the Full Fusion Page Instead

The Edit page's generators and packaged effects are convenient specifically because they're limited — a handful of exposed controls, not an editable graph. That's exactly the ceiling to watch for: once a shot needs several effects genuinely interacting with each other, or behavior no packaged effect exposes a control for, the honest move is opening the clip in the full Fusion page, where Part X's complete node-graph environment and toolset are actually available. The Edit page's versions aren't a lesser version of Fusion work; they're the right tool for contained, single-effect needs, with a clear, recognizable edge where a more complex composite genuinely belongs on the Fusion page instead.

The Same Building Blocks Underneath Everything

Stepping back, the throughline across this part so far is that transitions, titles, generators, and Fusion effects aren't four unrelated topics — they're variations on a small set of underlying building blocks: overlap, node-based composition, and elements that live on the timeline without necessarily coming from footage. A solid color matte used as a background, a gradient dropped in for a simple vignette, a title built in Text+ — all draw from the same toolkit this chapter has just named directly.

What's still missing from that toolkit is time itself — none of this part has yet covered how a property actually changes over the course of a clip rather than staying fixed. Chapter 7.5 closes out Part VII with keyframing, the mechanism that makes every tool covered so far capable of genuine motion rather than a single static state.

Key Concepts to Remember

  • A generator is any timeline element with no source footage behind it — solids, gradients, and titles are all one family.
  • Fusion effects on the Edit page are the same node technology as full Fusion, packaged into a few Inspector controls for speed.
  • Drop into the full Fusion page once a shot needs multiple interacting effects or behavior no packaged control exposes.
  • Transitions, titles, generators, and effects all draw from the same small set of underlying building blocks.
